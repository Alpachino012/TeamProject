Q: What is photosynthesis and what are its main stages?

A: Photosynthesis is a biological process by which green plants, algae, cyanobacteria, and certain other organisms convert light energy into chemical energy to fuel their metabolism [1,4]. During this process, light energy is captured and used to convert carbon dioxide, water, and minerals into oxygen and energy-rich organic compounds like carbohydrates [1,4]. Photosynthesis is a dual-stage process that consists of two main sets of reactions [5]. The first stage consists of the light-dependent reactions, which harvest energy and split water to release oxygen as a byproduct [1,5]. The second stage consists of the light-independent reactions, which use the harvested energy to build sugars [5].

Q: What were the main causes of the 2008 financial crisis?

A: The 2008 financial crisis was primarily triggered by the inflation and eventual collapse of the U.S. housing market [1,4,5]. This housing bubble was driven by cheap credit, loose lending practices, and excessive speculation on property values by both homeowners and financial institutions [1,4]. The crisis was further exacerbated by predatory subprime mortgage lending and significant deficiencies in regulation [1,2]. Additionally, Austrian school economists attributed the underlying issues to the Federal Reserve's prolonged policy of artificially low interest rates, which fostered a credit bubble, widespread malinvestment, and moral hazard [1].
